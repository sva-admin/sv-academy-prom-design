# Citations

Every empirical claim this repo makes, with its primary source. If a number
here cannot be traced to the study it names, that is a bug: open an issue.

## 1. People judge a page in about 50 milliseconds

**Claim, as used in the README:** people form a verdict on a page in roughly
50 ms, and they rarely revise it.

**Source:** Lindgaard, G., Fernandes, G., Dudek, C., & Brown, J. (2006).
*Attention web designers: You have 50 milliseconds to make a good first
impression!* Behaviour & Information Technology, 25(2), 115-126.
DOI: [10.1080/01449290500330448](https://doi.org/10.1080/01449290500330448)

**What the study actually did:** participants rated the visual appeal of web
pages shown for 50 ms and for 500 ms. Ratings at the two exposure times
correlated strongly, which is the basis for the "first impression forms almost
instantly" reading. The paper reports appeal judgments, not purchase decisions
or credibility ratings, and the follow-up finding is that these early
impressions are resistant to later revision.

## 2. Visual design is the most cited reason people give for trusting a site

**Claim, as used in the README:** asked why they did or did not trust a site,
people mentioned the look of the design more than anything else, in 46.1% of
comments, ahead of information structure at 28.5%.

**Source:** Fogg, B.J., Soohoo, C., Danielson, D., Marable, L., Stanford, J., &
Tauber, E.R. (2002). *How Do People Evaluate a Web Site's Credibility? Results
from a Large Study.* Stanford Persuasive Technology Lab / Consumer WebWatch.
Released 29 October 2002, updated 11 November 2002. Study term 15 June to
15 August 2002.
Full report (archived):
<https://web.archive.org/web/20120807142758/http://www.consumerwebwatch.org/pdfs/stanfordPTL.pdf>

The peer-reviewed conference version of the same study is Fogg et al. (2003),
*How do users evaluate the credibility of Web sites? A study with over 2,500
participants*, Proceedings of DUX '03.
DOI: [10.1145/997078.997097](https://doi.org/10.1145/997078.997097)

**What the study actually did:** 2,684 people evaluated two live sites on a
similar topic and wrote comments about each site's credibility. Researchers
coded 2,440 comments into categories. In the report's own words: "the design
look of the site was mentioned most frequently, being present in 46.1 percent
of the comments. Next most common were comments about information structure
and information focus." Information Design/Structure was 28.5%, Information
Focus 25.1%.

**Precision that matters:** the 46.1% is a share of *comments*, not a share of
*people*, and it measures what participants chose to mention, not a ranked
cause of trust. "Visual design is the most cited factor" is supported.
"46.1% of users judge credibility on design" is not what the study measured.

### The 75% number, and why it is not here

An earlier version of this README, along with a large amount of design writing
on the web, cited "about 75% of users judge credibility by visual design" and
attributed it to this same Stanford work. That figure does not appear in the
report. It is a misquote that has circulated widely enough to look sourced.

We replaced it with the figure the study actually reports. A skill whose whole
argument is that credibility is earned by details should not be making that
mistake in its own README.
